# Condo Detection & Analysis — Two Types, Two Outcomes

**Date: May 2026. CRITICAL CORRECTION: Condos ARE real estate — fee simple ownership of the unit + undivided interest in common elements. Do NOT treat condos as having $0 hard floor. The distinction is between FEE SIMPLE condos (own the unit) and LEASEHOLD condos (own nothing).**

## The Two Types of Condo Listings

| Type | Ownership | Hard Floor | Example | 
|------|-----------|------------|---------|
| **Fee Simple Condo** | You OWN the unit + common element share | Distressed market value of the unit (60-85% of ask) | 87 Jefferson St, Hoboken: $399K ask, $318K floor (80%) |
| **Leasehold Condo** | You own a lease + FF&E only | Equipment liquidation value (5-20% of ask) | 72-76 Market St, Clifton: $110K ask, $20K floor (18%) |

## Detection Checklist

### First: Fee Simple Condo or Leasehold?

1. **Look for "Lease" / "Option Term" / "Ground Lease" language** — if the listing mentions lease expiration dates, option periods, or ground leases → LEASEHOLD. Pass unless exceptional.
2. **"Condo Use" field**: "Investment or Owner User" → fee simple condo. "Lease assignment" → leasehold.
3. **Price per SF in context**: Hoboken $533/SF = market rate for fee-simple retail condo. Suburban NJ $91/SF = suspicious — probe deeper.
4. **"Price Per SF" matches market**: If $/SF is within 80-120% of market comps for the area, it's fee simple. If it's 30-50% below, it's likely a leasehold or business-only sale.

### Second: Unit vs Building (Condo Detection)

- **Fee Simple Condo**: Unit Size < Building Size (e.g., 748 SF unit in 5,000 SF building) — but the unit IS what you own
- **Leasehold**: Look for lease terms in description AND low price/SF

## Hard Floor Calculation — CORRECTED

### Fee Simple Condo
```
Hard Floor = Unit SF × Distressed Market PSF
```
- Hoboken retail condos: $400-500/SF distressed → 748 SF × $425 = $318K
- Typically 60-85% of ask for well-priced units
- This is REAL downside protection — you can always sell the unit

### Leasehold Condo
```
Hard Floor = Equipment Liquidation Value + Leasehold Premium
```
- Equipment: 20-40% of replacement cost at auction
- Leasehold: $0 unless assignable at below-market rent
- Typically 5-20% of ask — catastrophic downside

## When a Fee Simple Condo Is Worth Analyzing

A fee-simple condo like Jefferson St can be a good play because:
1. **Real downside protection** — 80% floor means you lose at most 20% in worst case
2. **Hoboken/urban location premium** — condos in top markets appreciate 5-8%/yr
3. **Owner-operator optionality** — you can lease it OR run your own business in it
4. **Business ideas analysis** — when vacant, evaluate 5-7 potential business concepts with revenue/NOI/capex estimates per concept

### Business Ideas Analysis (for vacant retail condos)

For each viable concept, estimate:
- Annual revenue (based on SF, location, market comps)
- NOI margin (25-35% for retail)
- Capex for buildout
- Cap rate on total cost basis (purchase + capex)
- Score 1-10 based on NOI/risk profile

**Constraints to check**: Liquor license eligibility, marijuana/smoke shop zoning, grease trap requirements, condo association use restrictions.

## When a Condo Is an Automatic PASS

1. **Leasehold** with <5 years remaining
2. **Fee simple but >$700/SF** in a non-trophy market
3. **Condo association in litigation or special assessment pending**
4. **Condo docs restrict the use you need** (verify before offer)
5. **No path to value-add** — purely a bet on market appreciation
